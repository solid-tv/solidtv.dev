The problems we get called about

TV hardware is years behind the phone in your pocket, and the app that flies on your laptop crawls on the device your viewers own. These are the usual suspects:

Scrolling stutters

Rows hitch as they move, usually from garbage collection in the render loop or too many nodes alive off screen.

Frames drop on older sets

The app holds 60fps on this year's flagship and 20fps on the five-year-old TVs most of your audience has.

Memory climbs until it crashes

Leaked textures and detached nodes that never show up on a desktop build take a Samsung set down in an evening.

The first screen jitters

Layout renegotiates after the first draw, so rows visibly jump into place on a 65-inch screen.

Startup is slow

A bundle that parses instantly on a laptop takes seconds on a TV chipset before anything is on screen.

Nobody can say why

Without on-device profiling and telemetry, every fix is a guess. We start by measuring.
